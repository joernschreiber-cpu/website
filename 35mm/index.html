35mm diary

„Film has a soul. Digital is math.“

There’s something beautifully irrational about loading a roll of film, manually cranking the lever, and knowing you’ve got exactly 36 shots to completely mess it up. Unlike digital, where you can instantly check the screen to reassure yourself, analog forces you to slow down. You actually have to think, guess the light, and commit to your decisions like an adult.

And then comes the absolute best and the worst part: the waiting. That special brand of uncertainty from the moment you hand over your roll at the lab until you finally get your hands on the negatives. Half of the shots will probably be out of focus, but that gap between hopeful anticipation and brutal reality is precisely why every decent frame feels like a major victory—and why this beautiful hassle feels like a real craft.
